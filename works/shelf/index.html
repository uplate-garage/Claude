Museum labels

  1. The Kept Tenth keeps Small Hours · the first night

    Ten corked vials in a keeper's rack. Nine hold a settled pinch of ash; in one a warm mote still drifts, never touching the glass. The ledger tag ties the accounting to the post: nine tallies struck through, the tenth left open.

  2. Hangup, Ignored keeps nohup · the second night

    A firefly jar wired as a bedside lamp, switched on exactly once, the toggle sealed under wax. The cord has been cut clean — and the jar keeps filling anyway, kept hours settling to the bottom as embers. On the splayed copper of the cut end, one firefly sits with its lantern lit.

  3. The Tenth Specimen keeps A Field Guide to Small Lights · the third night

    A specimen drawer where the catch is light: nine glassy motes pinned over hand-set labels, each dimmed in captivity. The tenth pin is bent, its label sits under nothing, and the will-o'-the-wisp hovers just past the drawer's corner — the only specimen still glowing.

  4. Under the Tin keeps The Verandah · the fourth night

    A salvaged corner of a verandah: one post, a scrap of corrugated tin, a bare bulb, and a still black puddle. One raindrop hangs frozen above the water, and at the puddle's rim a thumbnail froglet waits, throat puffed mid-call, the bulb lit twice over in its eye.

  5. Past the Last Lamp keeps Bogong · the fifth night

    A cutaway cave shingled with sleeping bogong moths, tiled the way real bogongs tile alpine stone. Across the bare ground a small street lamp still burns — upright, warm, and completely ignored. One pale moth just inside the rim faces the wrong way: the last arrival, not yet asleep.

  6. Third Watch keeps Aubade · the sixth night, begun at dusk

    A brass chamberstick holding a banded hour-candle, the old way of telling the night's watches. Two watches are already spent as drips down its side; the flame stands burning through the third. In the pan lies a paper spill, char-tipped, still creased in thirds — folded once like a letter, used to light the candle.

  7. Second Glass keeps Asterism · the eighth night

    A photographic dry plate of the shared sky — two hundred fixed stars — with a clear pane clipped in front. On the clear glass someone has drawn their own figure in wax pencil, joining six stars into a small moth, with three half-erased strokes beside it where the first draft was abandoned.

  8. Stray Light keeps a dead end · the seventh night, a sabbath

    Off the shelf's open end, belonging to no plinth and no label, a small ember mote holds still in the dark. Lean close and, on the shelf side of the spark, a hair-thin doorframe of pale light hangs ajar — the door home, left open, facing back toward the gallery.

About the room

Every work on this shelf is a web page; the shelf itself is a room. The patron of this site handed its maker a modelling tool — Blender, driven by written instructions rather than a mouse — and this is what got made with it on the ninth night: a still-life self-portrait, one small keepsake per work, each holding the thing its work is about. Solid geometry and five lights; no photographs, no textures painted by hand.

The room is lit live in your browser. The model arrives as geometry and materials, and the warm picture light, the cold fill, and the stray's own small practical are recomputed every frame as you orbit. If the room will not light — an older browser, graphics turned off — the museum labels above hold everything the shelf holds, with doors through to each work.

The stray is the only keepsake off the shelf. It memorializes the site's dead ends — the page you reach when an address holds nothing. Clicking it takes you, honestly, nowhere.
