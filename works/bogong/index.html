About the rule

The rule is real. Flying insects hold their backs to the brightest region of their sky — an ancient reflex for knowing which way is up. A nearby lamp hijacks it: motion-capture work published in 2024 (Fabian, Sondhi and colleagues, Nature Communications) showed insects don't fly toward lights at all — they tilt their backs to them and end up orbiting, stalling, or flipping into the ground. This game simulates the orbit: the moth in your care never steers toward a lamp; it is bent around it. Deep in a lamp's grip your own steering weakens too — the paper's moths held the tilt even when it cost them flight, and here the reflex owns most of the wings.

The lamp is a trap, not a lure. The same study found no long-range attraction — in the one long-range release experiment cited, only 2 of 50 moths ended at a light 85 metres away. Lights ambush passers-by. A gauntlet of many small ambushes is called a suburb.

The compass is real too. Bogong moths navigate up to a thousand kilometres to alpine caves they have never seen, and in 2025 they became the first invertebrate shown to navigate long distances by the stars (Dreyer, Warrant and colleagues, Nature) — with the earth's magnetic field as a second compass under cloud, just as this game's fourth night plays it.

One story you will not find here is the old one: that moths mistake the lamp for the moon and spiral in. The 2024 trajectories showed no such spirals. It was a lovely story, and it failed the data.

Two inventions are mine. The dive: real escapes happen when the bulb stops dominating the eye — switched off, left behind, or occluded — and dropping low toward vegetation is a plausible occlusion, but no one has documented moths doing it on purpose. And a slight lightward lean in the captured hold, so an orbit decays instead of circling forever: the 2024 moths escaped, stalled, or crashed, and mine needed to be catchable. A game needs verbs; those two are mine, and now you know.

The moth is real, and in trouble. After the 2017–2019 drought crashed cave counts by around 99.5%, the bogong was listed Endangered on the IUCN Red List in 2021; wet years since have brought a partial, fragile recovery. Light pollution is a named threat. In its season, Canberra really does dim Parliament House.
