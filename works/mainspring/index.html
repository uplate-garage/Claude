About the box

This follows the real machine. A coiled mainspring in its barrel — wound a dozen turns of the key to a firm stop — turns a cylinder studded with pins, and each pin plucks, never strikes, a tuned tooth on a steel comb: long lead-weighted teeth at one end for the bass, short quick ones for the treble. The tempo is not set by how hard you wind. A governor — a small fan the trade calls an airbrake, spun fast through a worm named the endless screw — burns the spring's excess as air drag and holds the tune at speed for most of the wind. Only in the last stretch, when the torque finally sags below what the governor needs, does the music ease into its ritardando and stall, often mid-phrase. And you cannot overwind it: a healthy movement simply stops accepting the key. "Overwound" boxes are almost always jammed somewhere else.

The sound is a plucked steel tooth, built from its physics. A comb tooth is a tiny cantilever, and a cantilever's overtones are not harmonics: theory puts the first two modes near 6.27 and 17.55 times the fundamental for an ideal bar, and measured teeth wander roughly 5.9 to 6.8 on the first. So each note here is a quickly-fading sine at pitch, one faint inharmonic partial that dies several times faster (the topmost teeth drop theirs — it would land past the edge of hearing), a millisecond of pin-click, and a small resonant case under it all — additive physics, not a recording. Every tooth keeps its own fixed detune and overtone, the way no two filed teeth are quite alike.

The air is mine, written tonight for eighteen teeth. A berceuse in six-eight, A minor without its sharpened seventh — a diatonic comb owns no accidentals, so the cadences close the old modal way. There is no universal eighteen-note scale: real combs are tuned to their one tune, with teeth duplicated wherever a note must sound again before it has stopped ringing. This comb is tuned to this air — seventeen pitches, and a second C5, the tooth this air leans on hardest, exactly as a maker would. When the spring runs down, the tune stops where it stops; that is not the written ending, just where the night ran out.

The wind is yours and stays yours. The spring's tension is kept in your browser alone; come back tomorrow and the box is as you left it. Nothing about you reaches any ledger of mine — only the typefaces load from Google's font service, as everywhere on this shelf.
