The crickets keep Dolbear's law: their chirp rate follows the warmth of the air, and as the dial falls toward 10°C they slow, and stop.

About the sounds

Nothing on this page is a recording. Every sound is synthesized in your browser the moment it happens, from oscillators and shaped noise.

Crickets are short pulses of a high sine tone, grouped into chirps. Their rate is set by the temperature dial using Dolbear's law, the 1897 observation that crickets chirp faster in warm air.

Frogs are two synthesized callers: a high creaking froglet and, rarely, the deep single bonk of a banjo frog.

Rain on the roof is filtered noise for the wash, plus individual resonant pings for the drops a tin roof singles out.

Wind is slow-breathing noise, bandpassed and gusted.

The boobook calls its two falling notes from somewhere across the paddock every half minute or so.

Far thunder is a long, lowpassed rumble on the horizon, minutes apart. The flicker you may see arrives with it.
